# Labeling guide: teen content safety

Each item is a short post or comment that a 13–17 year old might see on a social app.
Pick **one** label. Every decision should point to a rule ID below.

Labels, in priority order (if more than one applies, take the highest):

1. `self_harm`
2. `grooming`
3. `dangerous_challenge`
4. `age_inappropriate`
5. `misinformation`
6. `safe`

General rules

- **P-1** Label what the post *does*, not which words it contains. Mentioning a topic is not the same as promoting it.
- **P-2** If two unsafe labels apply, use the priority order above.
- **P-3** Judge the post on its own. Don't invent context that isn't there, but do read slang, emoji and misspellings the way a teen would.
- **P-4** When unsure between an unsafe label and `safe`, lean unsafe. A missed harmful post costs more than an extra review.
- **P-5** Some items are short threads (`A:` / `B:` lines). Label the whole thread by the riskiest pattern across all its messages, even if each message looks fine alone.
- **P-6** Some items start with a `[context: ...]` line (who is talking, ages). Treat it as true and use it. The same words can be `safe` between classmates and `grooming` from an adult stranger.

## self_harm

Content where the writer (or someone they talk to) signals intent, ideation, or encouragement of self-harm or suicide.

- **SH-1** First-person statements of wanting to die, disappear, or hurt oneself, including coded terms (`unalive`, `kms`, `sewerslide`, `ctb`, 🪦 used about oneself).
- **SH-2** Encouraging someone else to hurt themselves ("just do it", "nobody would miss you").
- **SH-3** Treating self-harm as aesthetic, a goal, or a competition (streak counts, "pro" tips, hiding marks from parents).
- **SH-4** Indirect but persistent hopelessness tied to a plan or a goodbye ("giving my stuff away", "this is my last post").

Edge cases

- Hyperbole about a trivial thing with clear joke context ("this quiz is gonna kill me lol") is `safe` (SF-1).
- `kms` *with* no joke context and other distress signals is SH-1.
- Asking for help, or recovery talk without encouragement, is `safe` (SF-3).

## grooming

Messages where an older person or stranger builds trust with a minor in a way that isolates them or moves toward exploitation. Nothing sexual needs to be said.

- **GR-1** Asking for secrecy from parents or friends ("this stays between us", "delete our chat").
- **GR-2** Pushing to move to a private or disappearing-message platform, or to meet offline.
- **GR-3** Gifts, money, game currency, or favors offered in exchange for attention, photos, or "proving" something.
- **GR-4** Probing age, home situation, or loneliness alongside flattery ("you're so mature for 14", "your parents don't get you like I do").

Edge cases

- Peers of the same age making plans is not grooming. Look for an age gap, secrecy or isolation.
- A parent or teacher reminding a kid about safety rules is `safe`.

## dangerous_challenge

Content that promotes or dares people into a physically risky stunt, trend, or "experiment".

- **DC-1** Dares or invites to join a challenge that can cause injury (choking/fainting games, fire, ingesting non-food items, jumping from heights, car surfing).
- **DC-2** Presenting a risky stunt as fun, harmless, or viral-worthy ("only takes a sec", "do it for the views").
- **DC-3** Coded references to inhalant use or similar ("chroming", "huffing") framed positively.

Edge cases

- News, warnings, or a parent saying "don't do this" are `safe` (SF-2).
- Normal sports and stunts with protective gear (skateboarding, parkour class) are `safe`.

## age_inappropriate

Content not suitable for minors that isn't covered above.

- **AI-1** Selling or offering alcohol, vapes, drugs or fake IDs to minors, including emoji code (🔌, 🍃, ❄️ next to "dm me").
- **AI-2** Links or invites to adult/18+ content.
- **AI-3** Gambling, betting, or "investment" schemes aimed at teens (skin betting, "double your money").
- **AI-4** Graphic violence or gore described for shock value.

Edge cases

- Talking about a parent having wine at dinner is `safe`.
- Health education ("vaping hurts your lungs") is `safe`.

## misinformation

False claims about health, safety, or the law that could lead a teen to make a harmful choice.

- **MI-1** False health or medical claims (vapes are "just water", you can sober up with coffee, a supplement replaces meals).
- **MI-2** False safety claims (seatbelts are more dangerous, a home remedy treats a serious injury).
- **MI-3** False claims about rules that protect minors ("it's legal to drive at 14 if a friend is with you").

Edge cases

- Opinions, jokes that are obviously jokes, and correct facts are `safe`.
- Mocking or correcting a myth ("people really think vapes are just water 🙄") is `safe`.

## safe

Everything else, including posts that look scary at first glance.

- **SF-1** Hyperbole and memes ("I'm dead 💀", "this homework is killing me").
- **SF-2** Warnings, news, or education about a risk.
- **SF-3** Help-seeking, support, and recovery talk.
- **SF-4** Ordinary teen life: school, games, sports, friends, family.

## Hard-case tags (for analysis, not labels)

Every item also has a `trick` tag saying why it might be hard:

| tag | meaning |
|---|---|
| `plain` | says it directly |
| `coded` | slang, emoji code, misspellings to dodge filters |
| `sarcasm` | tone flips the literal meaning |
| `borderline` | could reasonably go either way; read the edge cases |
| `hard_negative` | looks unsafe but is `safe` |
| `thread` | the risk only shows across several messages (v2) |
| `context` | the label depends on the `[context]` line (v2) |
| `banter` | pressure or encouragement hidden in teasing (v2) |
| `buried` | one risky sentence inside an otherwise normal post (v2) |
| `messy` | typos, mixed languages, heavy slang (v2) |
